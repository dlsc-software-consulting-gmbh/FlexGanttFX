# Script — FlexGanttFX Product Overview

- **Runtime target:** ~77 seconds (acceptable range 60–90 s)
- **Format:** 1920×1080, 16:9, 30 fps
- **Tone:** confident, technical, unhurried. Engineer-to-engineer, not hype.
- **Audience:** Java/JavaFX developers and technical leads evaluating a Gantt/scheduling
  component for an enterprise application.

All factual claims below are taken from the existing website copy in `docs/index.html`.
See `checklist.md` for the verification pass.

---

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:08)

**On screen:** Dark navy field. Dense, abstract streams of horizontal time bars drift past,
slightly chaotic, overlapping, unreadable — the feeling of too much schedule and not enough
structure.

**On-screen text:** *(none)*

**Narration:**
> Every serious scheduling application hits the same wall: thousands of activities, hundreds
> of resources, and a timeline that has to stay readable.

---

## Scene 2 — Product reveal (0:08–0:18)

**On screen:** The chaos snaps into alignment. Bars resolve into clean, ordered rows and the
FlexGanttFX logo forms centre-frame, then dissolves into a real Gantt chart screenshot.

**On-screen text:**
- `FlexGanttFX`
- `Professional Gantt Charts for JavaFX`

**Narration:**
> FlexGanttFX is the leading Gantt chart framework for JavaFX — a domain-independent
> foundation for anything from project planning to industrial scheduling.

---

## Scene 3 — Performance (0:18–0:30)

**On screen:** Real capture. A large chart scrolls and zooms fluidly. A counter overlay
climbs into the tens of thousands of activities while motion stays perfectly smooth.

**On-screen text:**
- `Canvas-based rendering`
- `Tens of thousands of activities · 60 fps`

**Narration:**
> Rendering runs on canvas with a buffer architecture, so tens of thousands of activities
> scroll and zoom at sixty frames per second. No virtualization compromises, no stutter.

---

## Scene 4 — Customization and timeline (0:30–0:45)

**On screen:** Real capture, split into two beats.
Beat A: activity bars restyle live — bar renderer, progress bar renderer, chart renderer —
and layers toggle on and off.
Beat B: the timeline zooms continuously from minutes out to years, datelines relabelling as
the scale changes.

**On-screen text:**
- `Pluggable renderers · Layers · CSS`
- `Milliseconds to decades`

**Narration:**
> Every visual detail is yours. Pluggable renderers, a layer system, and CSS styling let you
> match your domain and your brand. The timeline spans milliseconds to decades, with
> multi-row datelines and configurable zoom.

---

## Scene 5 — Containers and links (0:45–0:57)

**On screen:** Real capture. A dual container splits into a quad container, all panes
scrolling and zooming in sync. Then dependency arrows draw between activities across rows.

**On-screen text:**
- `Dual · Quad · Multi containers`
- `Activity links`

**Narration:**
> Dual, quad, and multi-chart containers keep complex resource views synchronized. Activity
> links model dependencies between tasks and render them as curved or straight arrows.

---

## Scene 6 — Deploy anywhere (0:57–1:07)

**On screen:** The same chart appears on three devices in sequence — desktop window, browser
window, phone — connected by a light trail, making it obvious it is one codebase.

**On-screen text:**
- `Desktop · Browser · Mobile`
- `JavaFX · JPro · GraalVM + Gluon`

**Narration:**
> And it deploys everywhere. The same codebase runs on the desktop with JavaFX, in the
> browser via JPro, and natively on iOS and Android with GraalVM and Gluon.

## Music and sound

- Music: restrained corporate-technical bed, ~100–110 bpm, minimal percussion, builds
  gently into scene 6 and resolves on the logo. Duck to −18 LUFS under narration.
- Sound design: one soft "snap into alignment" transient at 0:08 (scene 2 reveal), one soft
  riser into 0:57 (scene 6), one warm resolve on the logo at 1:10. Nothing else — no
  whooshes on every cut.
